The brief

Four business units, one definition of a number

A manufacturing and distribution group was running four ERPs, two of them home-grown, across a plant network, a wholesale arm, a components business and a trading company. Consolidation took eleven working days, planners could not trust inventory positions, and each unit negotiated its own purchase orders with the same suppliers.

We ran the platform selection across three tracks — Oracle, open source and Microsoft Dynamics 365 — then delivered the chosen platform end to end: fit-gap workshops, conference room pilots, configuration, master-data cleansing, historical migration, integration to the MES and banking estate, a staged cutover and eight weeks of hypercare. Finance, supply chain, manufacturing and HR now close on the same calendar.

Delivery model: selection and design in a fixed-fee first phase, followed by a time-and-materials build across six phases. Go-live was staged business unit by business unit so that each wave could be stabilised before the next began.

Capabilities

One ledger, one plan, one set of numbers

Nine functional areas configured to group policy — shared across entities, localised where statute requires it.

Financial management & GL

Multi-ledger general ledger, AP, AR, fixed assets, cash management and a five-step close checklist per entity.

Procurement & vendor management

Requisition-to-pay with approval thresholds, contract pricing, supplier scorecards and three-way matching.

Inventory & warehouse

Real-time stock positions by bin and lot, cycle counts, replenishment min/max and landed-cost valuation.

Manufacturing & production planning

Bills of material, routings, MRP runs, capacity levelling and shop-floor reporting fed back from the MES.

Sales & distribution

Quotation to cash with price lists, credit limits, pick-pack-ship and EDI acknowledgement for key accounts.

Project accounting

Capital projects and maintenance work orders with budget, commitment, actuals and WIP revaluation.

Multi-entity consolidation

Four legal entities, intercompany elimination, minority interest and currency translation in one close.

Role-based security & audit

Segregation-of-duties rules, maker-checker posting, field-level permissions and an immutable change log.

Analytics & board reporting

Group P&L, working-capital and OTIF dashboards on a conformed model, refreshed hourly into Power BI.

Workflow

Six phases from selection to steady state

Every phase has an exit gate with named evidence. Nothing moves forward because a date is approaching — it moves forward because the gate has been signed off by the business owner and the PMO.

  • Fit-gap decisions recorded in a signed design authority log
  • Data quality scored weekly against a 14-point cleansing rule set
  • Cutover rehearsed twice — one technical, one business — before go-live
  • Hypercare measured on ticket volume, not on elapsed calendar time

    Platform tracks

    Three routes to the same outcome

    We evaluated three tracks against the same fit-gap catalogue and the same five-year cost model, so the recommendation was never a matter of taste.

    Track 01

    Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP

    The heavyweight track. We specify Oracle when multi-GAAP consolidation, sophisticated revenue recognition and a long, funded product roadmap matter more than speed or licence cost.

    • Ideal fit: multi-entity, multi-country groups with complex consolidation and statutory reporting.
    • Typical modules: GL, AP, AR, cash management, procurement, inventory, manufacturing and HCM add-ons.
    • Licensing model: per-user subscription bundled by module tier, with annual support uplift.
    • Implementation notes: configuration-led with minimal code; Visual Builder for extensions and OData for integration.
    • When we recommend it: regulated groups already on Oracle technology where audit depth outweighs time-to-value.
    CapabilityTrack fitNotes
    Multi-entity consolidation Yes Native ledger consolidation across 40+ currencies and ledgers
    Localisation & statutory tax Yes Country tax packs maintained by Oracle on a published cadence
    Manufacturing & MRP depth Yes Discrete, process and mixed-mode planning with constraint support
    Low-code extension tooling Yes Visual Builder and BI Publisher keep custom work upgrade-safe
    Licence cost predictability Tiered subscription Predictable per user, but rises with module and role count
    Self-hosted deployment Oracle EBS only Fusion is SaaS; on-premise requires the E-Business Suite track
    Track 02

    Open source (Odoo 17)

    The value track. One application server, every module on the same object model, source code in your own repository and hosting wherever your compliance team is comfortable placing it.

    • Ideal fit: cost-sensitive mid-market groups wanting fast time-to-value and full source access.
    • Typical modules: Sales, inventory, MRP, purchase, accounting, projects and HR on a single ORM.
    • Licensing model: Community is free; Enterprise is a per-user subscription with hosting freedom.
    • Implementation notes: app-level configuration, Studio customisation, ORM/XML extensions and versioned upgrades.
    • When we recommend it: organisations with in-house engineering appetite to own upgrades long term.
    CapabilityTrack fitNotes
    Time to first go-live Yes Fastest of the three — 8 to 12 weeks for a first entity
    Source code access Yes Full repository, your fork, your release pipeline
    Self-hosted / private cloud Yes Any Linux host, Docker or Kubernetes, no vendor lock-in
    Third-party app ecosystem Yes Thousands of community and partner modules
    Localised invoicing (MENA / EU) Community packs Quality varies by country — validated before commitment
    Deep multi-GAAP consolidation Add-on or group reporting Solid for four entities; thinner than Oracle at group scale
    Track 03

    Microsoft Dynamics 365

    The standardisation track. ERP that lives inside the same identity, reporting and support boundary as the rest of the Microsoft estate — attractive when IT is already all-in on Azure.

    • Ideal fit: Microsoft-standardised estates that want ERP inside the M365 and Azure identity boundary.
    • Typical modules: Finance, Business Central, Supply Chain Management, Sales, Field Service and HR.
    • Licensing model: per-user plan or per-app licensing through an existing Microsoft agreement.
    • Implementation notes: dual-write and dataflows for integration, Power Platform for extensions, AL for ALM.
    • When we recommend it: organisations standardised on Azure with Power BI as the default reporting layer.
    CapabilityTrack fitNotes
    Fit with Microsoft 365 Yes Entra ID sign-off, Teams approvals, Outlook document handling
    Native Power BI reporting Yes Published data entities remove most of the extract work
    Business Central for SME units Yes Lighter footprint for smaller trading entities
    Partner ecosystem Yes Broad regional coverage with certified implementation partners
    Global statutory localisation 150+ countries Strong coverage; verify MENA e-invoicing roadmaps per country
    Hybrid on-premise option Business Central only Finance & Supply Chain Management is cloud-first

    Boring where it must be, modern where it pays
