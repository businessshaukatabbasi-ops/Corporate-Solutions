The brief

One platform for 10,000 people in the same browser tab

A training and certification organisation was running its instructor-led programmes on three separate white-labelled webinar tenants, each with its own vendor contract, dial-in bridge and desktop client. Corporate learners on managed laptops could not install the required plugins, and town-hall broadcasts ran four to eight seconds behind the presenter.

We designed and delivered a single self-hosted conferencing and webinar platform: WebRTC meetings and screen share, a broadcast mode for 10,000 concurrent viewers at sub-300ms glass-to-glass latency, cloud recording with transcription, breakout rooms, chat, Q&A and polls — all under three tenant brands with their own theme, domain and reporting.

Delivery model: fixed-scope build in eight months, then continuous monthly releases. Every launch is preceded by a 12,000-concurrent load test and a blue/green cutover, so a live town hall never waits on a deployment window.

    Run the room from a laptop, a phone or a lectern

    Co-hosts and presenters are frequently mobile — on a training floor, between classrooms or travelling. The host console is a progressive web app sharing the same permission model as the desktop console, so nothing is lost when the room moves.

    • Join from any modern browser — no app store, no plugin, no admin rights
    • Host controls mirrored on mobile: mute, spotlight, lock, record, remove
    • Live viewer count, bitrate and region health at a glance
    • Push notifications for session start, reminders and recording-ready events
    • Offline-tolerant chat that queues messages on flaky networks
    • Biometric unlock and device binding for tenant-branded builds

    Technology

    Chosen for latency, predictability and long service life

    Live media punishes guesswork, so the stack favours proven real-time components, media nodes that scale horizontally before a town hall, and boring, well-understood infrastructure everywhere else.

    Non-negotiables: DTLS-SRTP encryption on every hop, TURN for restrictive corporate firewalls, a hard 300ms latency budget verified in CI, and recorded capacity tests at 1.2× forecast before any major event.
